# Define a coherent art direction

Use when designing or rescuing a screen. Create a compact design brief before implementing major visual changes; don't bury this in a large speculative document.

## Design brief decision fields

- **Audience & primary job:** who arrives, what they want, how frequently, what they need to trust.
- **Archetype:** creative tool / operational dashboard / editorial landing / learning experience / commerce / data console; mixed products may need distinct marketing vs app surfaces.
- **Design thesis:** 1–2 sentences capturing visual personality plus functional priority.
- **One signature:** distinctive typography, composition, imagery, color contrast, or interaction pattern. Not five simultaneous signatures.
- **Type:** purposeful display/body hierarchy, realistic line lengths (~45–75 characters for reading copy), scalable sizes and vertical rhythm.
- **Layout:** page frame, sidebars/header, column logic, max content widths, gutters, grid alignment, density and responsive collapse rules.
- **Color roles:** background, surfaces, text, muted, divider, primary action, status/semantic colors; focus on contrast and clear meaning. Prefer existing brand token system.
- **Space/radius/elevation:** small coherent scales, not 17 arbitrary values. Use tokens only when repeated.
- **Media/art:** authentic product screenshots, relevant illustration, editorial photography, accessible charts; avoid decorative stock imagery when it adds no value.
- **Motion and interaction identity:** a consistent choreographic language, potentially bold typography, SVG, 3D-like depth, layered graphics or scroll narratives when suitable. Motion must not mask weak static composition.
- **Voice:** short verbs, meaningful headings, real tasks, no invented claims, no vague "Unlock the future" filler.

## Decision examples by product

- **Data-heavy command center:** prioritize scannable data, keyboard paths, density control, strong status labels, consistent tables and charts; effects are rare.
- **AI creation workflow:** emphasize input → AI process visibility → editable result → confidence/approval, plus transparency and undo; progressive disclosure beats a wall of cards.
- **Educational product:** make the next lesson/task obvious; progress and encouraging feedback without childish decoration; design for uninterrupted reading/speaking.
- **Marketing/brand landing page:** one specific proposition, crisp proof, meaningful demo visuals, restrained narrative rhythm and a clear call to action.

## Stop weak aesthetic defaults

Before committing to a direction, ask: could this be any unrelated SaaS if the logo changed? Is there a reason for every visual treatment? Are hierarchy and content doing more work than gradients? Is spacing systematic, not just "more whitespace"? Is the flagship screen recognizable at 50% zoom and usable at 200% zoom?

A cohesive, visually ambitious interface is better than a dense collage of unrelated components. Bold interactive experiments are welcome when the product and audience justify them; simplicity is not a universal aesthetic restriction. Do not default to black + neon or white + purple gradient without a product-specific reason.
